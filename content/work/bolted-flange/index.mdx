The course gave us a real load case: bolt a steam turbine's low-pressure exhaust hood to its water-cooled condenser, with stainless SA-193 B8 bolts through a 304L flange plate and axial load only. The joint can't separate, can't yield the bolts, and has to live with the load cycling.

We worked it the way Shigley lays it out. Edge distance and bolt spacing set the geometry. Bolt and member stiffness gave the share of the load each bolt actually sees, preload went to 75% of proof strength, and we took the minimum bolt count from the proof check. Then a spreadsheet stepped up the bolt count until every check cleared at once: proof load, joint separation, load factor, Goodman fatigue, and bearing on the flange.

<Figure name="bolt-hardware" caption="The bolt, nut and washer stack." narrow />

That landed on 28 bolts, 1-1/2 in. UNC Grade B8, each with a nut and two wide washers: five up each side of the flange and nine across the top and bottom.
