## The problem

Wind and solar don't produce on demand, so the grid needs somewhere to park the surplus. Terrament's answer is gravity: heavy modules ride a rail bolted to the wall of a deep mine shaft, climbing on motors when there's power to spare and generating on the way back down as those same motors brake them. The company pitches it as pumped hydro without the hill or the water. The concept, the patents and every performance figure on its site are Terrament's.

My part was the hardware, at 1/24 scale, and then the CAD for the mechanism that replaced it. The three models below are the three generations in the order I worked on them.

## First: the geared module

The module in Terrament's patent (US 12,272,947) climbs a rack on three geared wheels, with motors in the outer two and a cycloidal reduction in the one in the middle. I modeled the motor assembly, the gear drive and the bearings in Fusion 360, then assembled the prototype from printed gears and racks, off-the-shelf pillow-block bearings and an aluminum extrusion tower. I wired the motors and wrote the Arduino code that runs them.

<Model name="geared-module" caption="Generation 1 is the geared drive module, from my Fusion 360 assembly." />

It was built for Newlab's New Climate Futures event in September 2023, and getting there took rework. The final model has a corrected shaft hub, a second axle cap, a new double bearing, and new top and bottom bases.

<Video name="module-animation" caption="I made this exploded-view animation of the geared module in Fusion 360. It runs 25 seconds with no sound." />

I also ran the numbers, for the geared module and for the redesign that replaced it. That meant load and gear-ratio calculations and structural feasibility checks in Fusion 360, plus FEA for stress distribution and fatigue life that fed the choice between metal and plastic parts.

## Why it changed

The geared module worked at 1/24 scale, but a full-size one has to be installed down a mine shaft. The redesign aimed at two things: moving the weight more efficiently, and fitting a full-size shaft better. It traded the rotating geared wheels for a linear harmonic drive, where the module carries pins or rollers that step along a profiled rail.

On the redesign I worked from SketchUp sketches to Fusion 360 models, calculated the pin gap, pin sizes and cycling ratios, and 3D-printed a small proof of concept.

## Third: the linear harmonic drive

The last version moves the drive into the module. A motor turns a belt on two drive wheels, the belt carries a column of rollers that engage a cam-profiled I-beam rail, and ballast castings supply the weight. This is the July 2024 layout.
